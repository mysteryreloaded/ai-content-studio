# Site terms

The Studio can gate **everything** behind one set of site-wide terms: a
consent screen every user accepts once before using the Studio. It is
optional — if you never set site terms, users sign in and use the
Studio with no consent step of this kind (per-Model terms still apply,
see [the consent screen](consent.md)).

When set, the page is public at `/terms` and always linked from the
footer. This doc is the Operator's side; what the user actually sees is
in [the consent screen](consent.md).

## How versioning works

The terms body carries a **version number** the Studio maintains for
you:

- The **first time** you save a non-blank body, it becomes version 1.
- If you **blank** the body, the number is kept, not reset; a later
  re-save keeps the same number.
- If the body is already non-blank and you tick **"Ask everyone to
  accept again"** when you save, the version is **bumped** and
  **every user's acceptance is cleared** — on their next visit to a
  gated page they see the new version and must accept it.
- Any other save to a non-blank body — editing the wording without
  ticking *ask again* — **keeps the current version**: existing
  acceptances stay valid, and users simply see the updated wording
  next time they open the page.

Version numbers are never reused. The Audit log keeps, for every
change, the body before and after and whether *ask again* was ticked.

The admin's **Site settings → Terms** form has the body field, the
*ask again* checkbox and a **Save** button. A revision guard stops two
admins from clobbering each other's unsaved edits.

## What "gated" means

With site terms set, these pages require an acceptance that matches the
current version:

- the **active** pages (Workbench, Generations) and the **admin**
  pages;
- a **pending** user (not yet approved) sees the terms screen *first*,
  then the waiting page — a new user who hasn't been approved yet still
  has to accept before they sit on the waiting page.

A signed-in user who hasn't accepted the current version is redirected
to `/terms?return_to=…` and lands back on the page they wanted once
they accept. Accepting is `POST /api/terms/accept`; it records which
version the user accepted and when, and the Studio checks the current
version against that record on every gated route.

## A starter template

The Studio ships no terms text. This is a starting point to edit — and,
if your site generates content, to have a lawyer read:

> **Terms of use — <your site's name>**
>
> 1. **Who this is for.** This Studio is for users 18 or over (or the
>    age of majority where you operate). By signing in with your Google
>    account you agree to these terms.
> 2. **What you may do.** Generate images and videos using the Models
>    this Studio offers. Your results stay within the license of the
>    Model used — each Model's terms screen and license link state what
>    that means for you.
> 3. **What you may not do.** Do not generate or upload content that is
>    illegal in your jurisdiction, that infringes someone else's
>    rights, or that targets or harms a person. Do not use this Studio
>    to reverse-engineer, resell, or automate access to it.
> 4. **Your content is yours.** The Uploads and generations you create
>    belong to you. This Studio stores them on its own servers; you can
>    delete them any time, and they are removed automatically after the
>    retention period on the privacy page.
> 5. **Accounts.** Access may be approval-only before you can generate.
>    Accounts can be suspended for abuse; a suspended account can't
>    sign in.
> 6. **No warranty.** The service is provided as is. Content is
>    generated by AI Models and may be wrong, off, or offensive; you
>    are responsible for what you do with it.
> 7. **Changes.** These terms can change. When a change needs your fresh
>    acceptance, you'll be asked to accept the new version before you
>    can continue.

Adapt the age, the jurisdiction and the abuse rules to your site. If
you offer a Model with its own license (see
[Model licenses and AI rules](../licensing.md)), point users at it
here: the per-Model terms screen carries the specifics, but the site
terms are where the overall agreement lives.

**This template is not legal advice.** It is a reasonable starting
point for a small Operator; a lawyer in your jurisdiction should review
it before you rely on it.
